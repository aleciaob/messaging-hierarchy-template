# Pressure tests, build order and conflicts

Tier numbers refer to the [template](../template/hierarchy-template.md).

## How to pressure-test each tier

| Tier | Pressure test |
|---|---|
| 1 Core problem | Read it to three buyers without your product name. Do they finish your sentence? |
| 2 Category frame | Search the label. Do the results include the alternatives you expect? |
| 3 Positioning statement | Replace your name with a competitor's. Does it still read true? If yes, it is too generic. |
| 4 Boundaries | Ask a rep to list what the product does not do. Do their answers match the list? |
| 5 Naming map | Ask a new hire what they are selling. Count the different answers. |
| 6 Category sanity check | Ask two prospects what they filed you under before the call. Compare to your list. |
| 7 Benefit pillars | Cover the pillar names. Can a reader rebuild them from the proof alone? |
| 8 Differentiators | Hand each to a competitor's rep. Can they honestly claim it? |
| 9 Alternatives map | Show the "does well" column to someone who uses that alternative. Do they agree? |
| 10 Capability chain | Delete the last link. Does the middle link still make sense to a user? |
| 11 Anchor sentence | Read it cold to someone outside the company. Do they understand it? |
| 12 Segment messaging | Swap two segment names. How many sentences survive unchanged? |
| 13 Buyer-role messaging | Read the "what they say" quote aloud in a call. Does it sound like a real person? |
| 14 Proof library | For each row, ask what you would say if the buyer asked "what was the sample?" |
| 15 Objection responses | Role-play with the probing questions only. Does the buyer reveal the real concern? |
| Claim rules | Take five live pieces of copy. Check each against all rules. How many pass? |

## Build order

1. **Core problem (Tier 1).** Everything else depends on it.
2. **Buyer, segment and alternatives (Tiers 12 and 9, outline only).** Decide who you are for and who you are compared with before choosing words.
3. **Category frame (Tier 2).** Choose the label with your alternatives list in view.
4. **Proof inventory (Tier 14).** List what you can actually prove before you write any benefit.
5. **Boundaries and claim rules (Tier 4 and the [claim rules](../template/claim-rules.md)).** Set limits before copy is written.
6. **Differentiators (Tier 8).** Keep only what survives the "can a competitor say it?" test and has proof.
7. **Benefit pillars (Tier 7).** Derive them from differentiators and proof, not the other way round.
8. **Capability chain (Tier 10).** Fill in once pillars are fixed.
9. **Positioning statement and naming map (Tiers 3 and 5).** Now that the substance is settled, write the compact forms.
10. **Anchor sentence (Tier 11).** Last of the core tiers, because it summarizes the rest.
11. **Applied messaging (Tiers 12, 13 and 15).** Build from the core outward.
12. **Category sanity check (Tier 6).** Run it as a final test on the finished hierarchy.

## What to do when tiers conflict

- **Claim rules beat everything.** If a differentiator, pillar or proof item breaks a rule, the item changes, not the rule.
- **Proof beats pillars.** If a pillar has no proof, soften it or cut it. Do not invent proof to hold a pillar up.
- **Company level beats segment level on category and claims.** Segment level wins on emphasis, examples and vocabulary.
- **The master document beats the derived one.** Name which document is the master. When the sales version and the marketing version disagree, the sales version is edited.
- **When two rules collide,** the more restrictive rule applies until the named owner decides in writing.
- **When a rep says the rule costs deals,** log the case with the deal details. Review the log on a fixed cadence. Change the rule only through the owner, never through exceptions in the field.
- **When the product changes,** re-run Tiers 4, 7, 8 and the claim rules first. Everything else follows from them.
